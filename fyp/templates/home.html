Medical Background

Understanding Diabetes

Diabetes is a chronic metabolic disease characterized by elevated blood glucose levels. There are three primary types, each with distinct causes and risk profiles.

Type 1 Diabetes
Autoimmune — Insulin Dependent
The immune system attacks insulin-producing beta cells in the pancreas. Patients require lifelong insulin therapy. Onset typically in childhood or early adulthood.
Affects ~8% of all diabetics
Genetic and environmental triggers
Not preventable currently
Type 2 Diabetes
Metabolic — Insulin Resistant
The body fails to use insulin effectively (insulin resistance). Most common form, strongly linked to lifestyle, obesity, and age. Often silent for years before diagnosis.
~90% of all diabetes cases
Preventable and manageable
This tool screens for Type 2
Gestational Diabetes
Pregnancy-Related — Temporary
Develops during pregnancy when hormonal changes cause insulin resistance. Usually resolves after birth, but significantly increases future risk of Type 2 diabetes.
Affects 2–10% of pregnancies
Managed with diet and exercise
7× higher future T2D risk
Clinical Signs

Warning Signs to Watch

These classic symptoms of diabetes can appear gradually. Many people ignore them for months or years before seeking help.

Frequent Urination
Kidneys work overtime to filter excess sugar, causing you to urinate more frequently, especially at night.
Excessive Thirst
Persistent thirst even after drinking water is a hallmark sign as blood sugar draws fluid from tissues.
Unexplained Weight Loss
Without insulin to move glucose into cells, the body burns fat and muscle as alternative energy sources.
Blurred Vision
High blood sugar causes the lens of the eye to swell, changing its shape and reducing visual clarity.
Extreme Fatigue
Cells starved of glucose cannot produce adequate energy, causing persistent tiredness unrelieved by rest.
Slow-Healing Wounds
Elevated blood glucose impairs circulation and immune response, causing cuts and sores to heal very slowly.
Numbness / Tingling
Diabetic neuropathy begins with tingling in hands and feet as nerves become damaged by prolonged high blood sugar.
Recurring Infections
High glucose feeds bacteria and fungi, making diabetics more prone to skin, urinary tract, and yeast infections.

Our Technology

How It Works

A four-step pipeline from data entry to machine learning prediction — designed to be fast, accurate, and easy to use.

01
Enter Your Data
Fill in your personal, lifestyle, medical history, and clinical measurements through our guided 3-step form.
02
Data Processing
Our Flask backend receives your data, applies standard scaling and preprocessing — just like a trained lab pipeline.
03
ML Prediction
A Random Forest model trained on the PIMA Diabetes Dataset analyzes your 8+ features and returns a risk probability.
04
Your Results
Receive a detailed risk score, key factor analysis, and personalized recommendations — all in under 3 seconds.
Random Forest Classifier
An ensemble of decision trees trained on the PIMA Indians Diabetes Dataset with 768 samples and 8 clinical features. Handles class imbalance via balanced weights.
~77% Accuracy on test split
8 Clinical Features
Glucose, Blood Pressure, Insulin, BMI, Age, Pregnancies, Skin Thickness, and Diabetes Pedigree Function — all validated features from clinical research.
10-Fold Cross-validation used
Preprocessing Pipeline
Zero-value imputation using column medians, followed by StandardScaler normalization — ensuring the model receives clean, properly scaled input every time.
Sklearn Pipeline + Pickle serialization
